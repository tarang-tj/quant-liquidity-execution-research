# Regime-Aware Liquidity-Risk Optimal Execution

## 1. Project contract

**Objective.** Build a reproducible research prototype that liquidates a fixed parent order over a short horizon while balancing temporary market impact against inventory risk. The model updates its execution schedule as evidence of a latent *calm* or *stressed* liquidity regime arrives.

**Scope.** This is a synthetic-data research project, not a trading system, investment recommendation, or claim of historical profitability. The simulated market process is deliberately controlled so that the latent state is known ex post for validation.

**Research questions.**

| ID | Question | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| q1 | Can a causal two-state Bayesian filter infer a changing liquidity regime from volatility, spread, and displayed-depth observations? | posterior probabilities, classification diagnostics, calibration/error summary |
| q2 | Does an execution schedule that uses the posterior regime reduce tail implementation shortfall versus static schedules under liquidity stress? | schedules, constraint checks, Monte-Carlo mean and CVaR shortfall comparison |
| q3 | Can completed batches recalibrate the filter without contaminating the next independent evaluation batch? | rolling parameter snapshots, out-of-sample filter score, and feasibility-checked learned-policy tail cost |

The only source data will be a seeded synthetic intraday market generator written within this project. Inputs are observed before a decision; the true regime is retained only for q1 validation and is not given to the decision rule.

## 2. Inputs, outputs, and assumptions

### 2.1 Inputs

| Input | Symbol | Units | Source / use |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Parent sell order | $Q$ | shares | Fixed target inventory to liquidate |
| Time buckets | $T$ | 10-min intervals | 13 decision intervals in a 130-minute horizon |
| Regime state | $z_t \in \{0,1\}$ | calm / stressed | Latent Markov state used by generator only |
| Observations | $y_t=(\log \sigma^{\mathrm{trail}}_t,\log s_t,\log d_t)$ | standardized | causal inputs to Bayesian filter |
| Temporary-impact scale | $\eta(z_t)$ | bps per squared fraction of parent order | higher in stressed state |
| Volatility | $\sigma(z_t)$ | bps per interval | inventory-risk scale |
| Executable participation cap | $c_t$ | fraction of $Q$ | observed at interval start from displayed depth |

### 2.2 Decision and outputs

At each interval the policy allocates the fraction $v_t=x_t/Q$ of the parent order, subject to

$$v_t\ge0,\qquad v_t\le c_t,\qquad \sum_{t=1}^T v_t=1.$$

The policy uses the filtered stress probability $p_t=P(z_t=1\mid y_{1:t})$ and its Markov forecasts for subsequent intervals to form expected impact and volatility; the current cap $c_t$ is directly observable rather than inferred. At each replan, future capacities are conservatively set to the known DGP floor $\underline c=0.09$, not to future realized values. It then solves a convex, receding-horizon execution problem. Results report trade schedules, realized implementation shortfall, mean shortfall, 95% CVaR, constraint feasibility, paired common-random-number contrasts, and bootstrap confidence intervals across seeded Monte-Carlo paths.

### 2.3 Modeling assumptions and limits

1. One asset, sell-side execution, no alpha forecast, no fees, and no permanent impact in the base model.
2. Regime transitions are stationary in the generator. This is a controlled assumption, not a calibration claim.
3. Observed spread, depth, and trailing volatility are conditionally Gaussian after log transformation; the filter parameters are known in the base experiment to isolate decision quality from estimation error.
4. The convex impact approximation is appropriate for a portfolio-quality demonstration but not a production execution stack.
5. A static Almgren–Chriss-style schedule and a front-loaded risk-aware schedule are baselines. The primary comparison is policy evaluation under identical simulated paths.

## 3. Model design

### 3.1 q1: latent liquidity-state filter

At the start of interval $t$, the Markov state $z_t$ is fixed but hidden and the quote/trailing-volatility observation $y_t$ is available. The policy filters $p_t$, chooses $v_t$, and only then the price shock and realized cost for interval $t$ occur. No $y_{t+1:T}$, price shock, or true state is available to the policy. The market state follows a two-state Markov chain with transition matrix $P$. With emission density $f_k(y_t)$ for state $k$, the causal posterior is updated by

$$\tilde p_t(k)=\sum_j P_{jk}p_{t-1}(j),\qquad
p_t(k)=\frac{f_k(y_t)\tilde p_t(k)}{\sum_{\ell}f_{\ell}(y_t)\tilde p_t(\ell)}.$$

This family is selected because it makes the partially observed state explicit, produces a directly interpretable stress probability, and preserves causal information flow. An EWMA spread/volatility score was considered as the sole detection model but rejected as the primary model: it cannot represent transition persistence or a coherent posterior. It will not be counted as a second optimized model family.

**q1 success criteria.** The code must verify posterior values lie in $[0,1]$, probabilities sum to one, and the posterior is computed before that interval’s execution decision. Diagnostics include balanced accuracy, Brier score, log loss, and a posterior-versus-true-regime timeline.

### 3.2 q2: posterior-conditioned convex execution

All objective terms are normalized to **basis points of parent-order notional**. Let $r_t=1-\sum_{j< t}v_j$ be remaining parent-order fraction. At decision time $t$, set $p_{t+k\mid t}=P(z_{t+k}=1\mid y_{1:t})$ by propagating the filtered distribution through $P^k$. Use $\bar\eta_{t+k}=(1-p_{t+k\mid t})\eta_0+p_{t+k\mid t}\eta_1$ (bps per squared parent-order fraction) and $\bar\sigma_{t+k}^2=(1-p_{t+k\mid t})\sigma_0^2+p_{t+k\mid t}\sigma_1^2$ (bps$^2$ per interval). For remaining fraction $r_t$, choose $v_{t:T}$ by minimizing

$$
\min_v \sum_{\tau=t}^T \bar\eta_\tau v_\tau^2 +
\lambda\sum_{\tau=t}^T \bar\sigma_\tau^2\left(r_t-\sum_{j=t}^{\tau}v_j\right)^2,
$$

subject to $0\le v_t\le c_t$, $0\le v_\tau\le\underline c=0.09$ for $\tau>t$, and $\sum_{\tau=t}^T v_\tau=r_t$. Thus only the contemporaneously observed $c_t$ is used; future realized caps remain hidden until their own interval. Here $\lambda$ has units $1/\mathrm{bps}$, so $\lambda\bar\sigma^2r^2$ is bps and is commensurate with impact. The first child order is executed after filtering, then the price shock is realized and the horizon rolls forward. The strictly convex, small-dimensional quadratic program is solved by a deterministic active-set method over equality-constrained box faces, with exact KKT solves and post-solve constraint validation. If the known current-plus-floor capacity $c_t+(T-t)\underline c<r_t$, the implementation must halt as infeasible rather than silently exceeding a cap.

**Baselines.**

| Policy | Information used | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| TWAP | no state information | transparent execution baseline |
| Static AC-like | unconditional impact/volatility parameters | conventional risk-impact baseline |
| Regime-aware MPC | filtered posterior only | proposed policy |
| Oracle MPC | full latent-state path | non-deployable diagnostic lower bound only; excluded from policy claims |

**q2 success criteria.** Every path must finish $Q$ shares within tolerance, respect non-negativity/caps, and preserve inventory accounting. For a realized path, implementation shortfall is $\mathrm{IS}=\sum_t\eta(z_t)v_t^2+\sum_t r_{t+1}\epsilon_t$, in bps, where $r_{t+1}=r_t-v_t$ and $\epsilon_t\sim N(0,\sigma(z_t)^2)$ is the post-decision price shock. Policy evaluation uses common random numbers across policies, at least 1,000 paths in the full run, and reports the distribution—not merely a point estimate—of shortfall.

### 3.3 Fixed synthetic data-generating process

The full run uses the following fixed baseline; every parameter is also emitted to `results/复现清单.json`.

| Component | Calm ($z=0$) | Stressed ($z=1$) | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Markov transition $P$ | $P_{00}=0.94$ | $P_{11}=0.82$ | remaining entries are complements |
| Emission mean $\mu_z$ for $(\log\sigma^{\mathrm{trail}},\log s,\log d)$ | $(1.70,0.10,11.42)$ | $(2.10,0.45,11.10)$ | trailing volatility in bps, spread in bps, depth in shares |
| Emission covariance $\Sigma$ | $\mathrm{diag}(0.20^2,0.25^2,0.28^2)$ | $\mathrm{diag}(0.28^2,0.32^2,0.35^2)$ | independent conditional features in baseline; deliberately overlapping emissions make filtering non-trivial |
| Post-decision price volatility $\sigma_z$ | 5 bps | 10 bps | drives $\epsilon_t$ |
| Temporary impact $\eta_z$ | 7 bps | 24 bps | cost coefficient in IS formula |
| Observed capacity | $c_t=\max(0.09,\min(0.18, 0.12\,d_t/10^5))$ | same mapping | $d_t$ is observed before trade; $T\times0.09=1.17$ gives a pathwise completion buffer |
| Parent order / horizon | $Q=100{,}000$ shares | $T=13$ | shares only convert fractional schedule to child orders |
| Risk aversion | $\lambda=0.08\;\mathrm{bps}^{-1}$ | — | sensitivity grid: 0.02, 0.08, 0.20 |

The generator writes both the observable emissions and the withheld $z_t$ to the input CSV. $z_t$ is used only for q1 scoring and realized-cost accounting after the policy decision; it never enters the policy feature matrix.

## 4. Validation and stress plan

1. **Filter validity:** use independently seeded synthetic evaluation paths with known data-generating parameters; compare latent states to causal posterior classification. No fitted training split is used, and no future observations enter the filter.
2. **Execution validity:** run each policy on the same market paths and calculate realized cost from the state-dependent market-impact process.
3. **Paired inference:** exploit shared market paths to bootstrap paired policy-cost differences, rather than treating policy results as independent samples.
4. **Oracle diagnostic:** compare the causal policy with an intentionally non-deployable full-state oracle to quantify the remaining value of state information; never present it as a feasible strategy.
5. **Stress test:** increase transition probability into the stressed state and increase stressed-state impact; record changes in mean and tail cost.
6. **Sensitivity:** vary risk aversion $\lambda$ and impact-stress multiplier. The conclusion is limited to the simulated data-generating process.
7. **Failure handling:** infeasible participation caps cause a clear runtime error. Any solver constraint violation larger than $10^{-8}$ fails the run.
8. **Continual calibration (q3):** after a synthetic batch closes, its withheld state labels are used to re-estimate the Markov transition matrix and Gaussian emissions with Laplace-smoothed transitions and covariance shrinkage. The learned snapshot is then used only on the next independently seeded batch; that batch joins training history only after scoring. `results/online_filter_snapshot.json` records, for every cycle, the complete parameter arrays plus training/holdout seed and path-batch provenance. This is an auditable synthetic research protocol, not autonomous live trading or a claim that real-time labels are available in markets.
